4The maritime digest

Earlier on, most of the target-finding ran off a tool I built: the Maritime Signal Digest. It sweeps shipping, offshore and defence sources on a schedule and reads everything it finds against what we actually sell.

The brain does two things. First it asks whether there is a reachable buyer in the story at all, and whether they have a fresh, concrete reason to want better ocean-current data. Then it ranks what survives by need: at the top sit jobs gated by a written weather limit, where a day spent waiting burns the day rate; below that, work on a hard window, where a missed laycan can kill the fixture; below that, margin plays.

It watches for a handful of triggers: a campaign starting with a dated window or a named vessel, a company admitting weather losses in its own results, a budget opening up for decarbonisation or digital operations, an asset moving to a new trade, a routing-tech adoption, a digital-ops hire.

What comes back is named companies with the reason for writing attached.
