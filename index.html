4The story

What we actually have. Heerema is a client on contract, with the Sleipnir and Aegir vessel models built and in use, and a second engineer there opened an account in August. SeaCras have signed an NDA and have an interested party behind them. Deltares held the meeting and an NDA is coming. ONE Line is confirmed in person on 9 September. Ship2Port hold the only priced offer on the table, ten thousand euros a year for the API and the same again for the visualisation layer. Willem Burger has agreed the introducer contract for the superyacht side, fifteen per cent of what a yacht he brings pays in its first eight months; it is not signed yet because we still need his tax residence and address and Alexandre's signature. And Amphitrite is in the PIER71 cohort, in Singapore from 6 to 12 September, with the finale on 11 November.

Where the leads came from. The 23 July batch was not guesswork. The maritime digest run of 20 July named Anduril, HII, Metron, Norwegian Cruise Line, Anglo-Eastern, Western Bulk and ONE Line, and those companies were written three days later. The digest has not run since 27 July. Its last two runs named companies nobody has written to yet: Cadeler, DEME, Fred. Olsen Windcarrier, GEOxyz, Ocean Winds, Kraken Robotics, Sonardyne, MSubs, SeaBird Exploration and ADES.

Singapore, honestly. Fifty-three emails, no replies, and six addresses bounced. A screen on 19 August keeps Berge Bulk, Swire, Hafnia, IMC and IINO, and cuts nine. Berge Bulk, Swire and Hafnia pass every test and carry twenty-six of the live emails, so for those three the silence is not the targeting. Britoil was cut because the charterer buys the fuel. That is what the standard offshore charter says, but only in a narrow sense: the clause reads "while the Vessel is on hire the Charterers shall provide and pay for all fuel". Off hire, on positioning runs between contracts, and when buying back the bunkers at redelivery, the owner pays. Goodwood, Eaglestar and Yinson were cut because they do not control the voyage, which is the reason to go to the owner of the hulls they manage instead. That route is open and untried.

What the market told us. TORM thought we were a Mediterranean product. RWE already buys forecasting and decision-support tools. Ship2Port asked why they would pay for something built on data that is free. Those are the same objection in three forms, and each one now has an answer sitting in front of the person who raised it.

Someone else moved on our idea. DSNM put an ocean-currents layer into Compass around 17 August, announced by their Chief Operating Officer: current direction and speed overlaid on the planned route, expected values in the weather table and in the passage synopsis. Our proposal to supply exactly that layer went to them on 4 August and has not been answered. The announcement does not say whose data is underneath.

Next. The follow-ups now due, in one list: Hiscox, Steamship Mutual, Convex, Tokio Marine Kiln, Canopius, Camera Law, Varamar, YachtEye, TORM, DSNM Compass, Ship2Port, Explorers Hub, MPA Singapore, Breakbulk Europe 2027 and Aaudaz Maritime.
